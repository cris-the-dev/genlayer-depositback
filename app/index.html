How it works

Before and after decide.

  1. I
    Open

    The landlord names the tenant, the property, the deposit, the end of the rental and the check-in report on an approved inspection site. Its hash is pinned now.

  2. II
    Pay

    The tenant sends the exact deposit into escrow. Until then the landlord can cancel.

  3. III
    Claim

    In the 14 days after the rental ends, the landlord may make one claim: an amount, the damage, and the check-out report. Validators agree whether the damage is in the check-out report and not in the check-in report.

  4. IV
    Return

    Valid pays the claim to the landlord. Then, or after 14 days with no claim, anyone sends the rest to the tenant.

Built for both sides

For tenants

  • The deposit is in escrow, not spent.
  • Wear that was there at check-in can't be charged.
  • One claim, in 14 days; after that anyone returns your money.

For landlords

  • Recorded damage is paid without a dispute service.
  • The deposit is in escrow before the keys are handed over.
  • The ruling quotes the report, so there's nothing to argue about.
Independent reportsBoth reports must be on an approved inspection host, not on a page the landlord controls. The check-in report is pinned by hash when the deposit opens.
Quoted, not guessedA valid claim needs a sentence from the check-out report that every validator finds in its own copy.
Honest limitsValidators judge whether the damage is new and recorded, not what it costs to fix: the landlord names the amount, capped at the deposit.

FAQ

Who decides whether a claim is valid?

GenLayer validators. Each one reads both reports itself, checks the check-in is the pinned version, and answers: is the claimed damage in the check-out report and not already in the check-in report? A result counts only when they agree, and valid also needs a quote from the check-out report.

What if a report is down or edited?

The claim reverts and nothing changes. An edited check-in report never matches the pinned hash. If the landlord can't claim within 14 days, the deposit goes back to the tenant.

Can the landlord claim twice?

No. One claim, judged once. The rest goes back to the tenant.

Who sends the money back?

Anyone, once the claim is judged or the 14 days pass without one. The landlord can return it at any time. It always goes to the tenant.

Deposits without the argument.

Escrow it, pin the check-in, and let consensus compare the reports.
